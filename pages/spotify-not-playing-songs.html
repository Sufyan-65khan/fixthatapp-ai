Spotify Not Playing Songs: 8 Fixes for Greyed Tracks, Skipping & No Sound

Last updated: May 9, 2026 · Verified on Spotify Free and Premium across iOS, Android, desktop, and web

By Sufyan Khan — Founder, FixThatApp  |  Editorial standards

Spotify playback problems fall into several distinct categories that people often confuse with each other. Greyed-out songs are a licensing issue â€” those tracks genuinely aren't available in your region and no setting will fix them. Songs that skip immediately without playing are usually corrupted downloads or session token problems. Spotify pausing randomly is almost always another device or battery optimization killing the app. And no sound despite the song appearing to play is an audio routing problem â€” common when Bluetooth devices are involved.

Identifying which problem you have saves a lot of time, because the fixes are completely different.

8 Fixes for Spotify Not Playing Songs

Fix 1: Understand Why Songs Are Greyed Out

Why this matters: Greyed-out tracks on Spotify are not a bug â€” they're intentionally blocked in your region due to music licensing. The artist or their label hasn't licensed the song for streaming in your country. This is different from a playback error.

What you can do:

  • Look for alternate versions of the song â€” sometimes a remix, live version, or re-release is licensed in more regions.
  • Add the song to your library â€” some tracks become available when you do this, as Spotify occasionally updates licensing.
  • Check if Spotify has "local files" for that track â€” you can import your own MP3s into Spotify and they'll appear in your library for playback.

Note: songs recently added by artists sometimes appear greyed out for a few hours while Spotify's catalog updates â€” try again later.

Fix 2: Fix Songs That Skip Immediately Without Playing

Why this works: When Spotify skips a song the moment you tap it â€” without even starting playback â€” the most common cause is a corrupted download file or an expired authentication token that your session is still trying to use.

  1. Open Spotify and go to Settings (gear icon) â†’ Storage.
  2. Tap Delete Cache. This removes temporary files without affecting downloads.
  3. Close Spotify completely (force stop on Android, swipe away on iPhone).
  4. Reopen and try playing the song again.
  5. If it still skips, log out: Settings â†’ scroll to the bottom â†’ Log Out. Then log back in.

If you're playing an offline download that skips: go to the downloaded album or playlist, tap the three dots â†’ Remove Download, then re-download it on Wi-Fi.

Fix 3: Stop Spotify from Pausing Randomly

Why this works: The two most common causes of Spotify randomly pausing are: (1) another device is playing on your Spotify account simultaneously, and (2) Android's battery optimization is killing Spotify in the background to save power.

For multiple-device conflicts:

  1. Go to spotify.com in a browser and log into your account.
  2. Click your profile â†’ Account â†’ scroll to Sign Out Everywhere.
  3. Log back in only on your phone.

For Android battery optimization:

  1. Go to Settings > Battery (or Battery & Performance on some phones).
  2. Find Battery Optimization or App Battery Management.
  3. Find Spotify in the list and set it to Don't optimize or Unrestricted.
  4. On Samsung: Settings > Device Care > Battery > Background Usage Limits â€” make sure Spotify isn't on the sleeping apps list.

On iPhone: Go to Settings > Spotify and make sure Background App Refresh is enabled.

Fix 4: Fix "Playing But No Sound" Issues

Why this works: When Spotify's progress bar moves and it looks like a song is playing but you hear nothing, the audio is routing somewhere you're not hearing â€” usually to a Bluetooth device that's connected but out of range or off.

  1. Check the Spotify Connect device selector â€” tap the speaker icon at the bottom of the Now Playing screen. If it shows a Bluetooth speaker or TV, tap This Phone to redirect audio.
  2. On iPhone: swipe down from the top right to open Control Center â†’ tap the AirPlay icon on the music widget â†’ select iPhone.
  3. Turn off Bluetooth temporarily (Settings > Bluetooth > toggle off) to rule out a ghost Bluetooth connection.
  4. Check your phone's media volume â€” it's separate from the ringer. Press the volume up button while a song is "playing" in Spotify.
  5. If using headphones: unplug and replug them, or forget and re-pair Bluetooth headphones.

Fix 5: Clear Spotify's Cache

Why this works: Spotify maintains a large cache of album artwork, podcast episodes, and streaming data. When this cache grows corrupt â€” which happens over months of use â€” songs may fail to load, take extremely long to start, or show a loading spinner indefinitely.

  1. Open Spotify and tap the gear icon (top right on profile).
  2. Scroll down to Storage.
  3. Tap Delete Cache and confirm.
  4. Close Spotify fully and reopen it.

This is safe â€” it does not delete your downloaded songs or playlists. However, album artwork and recently streamed data will re-download over the next few minutes.

Fix 6: Fix Offline Downloads That Won't Play

Why this works: Spotify's offline downloads can get corrupted if the download was interrupted, or if you haven't connected to the internet for more than 30 days (Spotify requires periodic online check-ins to verify your Premium subscription before allowing offline playback).

  1. Connect to the internet (Wi-Fi works best).
  2. Open Spotify â€” it will re-verify your Premium status automatically.
  3. For a corrupted download: navigate to the album or playlist, tap the three dots â†’ Remove Download.
  4. Then tap the download button again to re-download on Wi-Fi.

If all your downloads stopped working at once, check that your Premium subscription hasn't lapsed at spotify.com/account.

Fix 7: Update the Spotify App

Why this works: Spotify updates frequently, and running an older version can cause playback issues â€” particularly after Spotify updates its streaming codec or DRM (copy protection) system on the server side, which can make older app versions incompatible.

On iPhone: Open the App Store â†’ tap your profile icon â†’ scroll to Spotify â†’ tap Update.

On Android: Open Google Play Store â†’ tap your profile â†’ Manage apps & device â†’ find Spotify â†’ tap Update.

On desktop (Windows/Mac): Click the three dots in the top left of Spotify â†’ Help â†’ About Spotify â†’ click the update link if shown. Alternatively, download the latest version from spotify.com.

Fix 8: Log Out of All Devices and Log Back In

Why this works: Spotify's authentication tokens can become stale, particularly if your password was recently changed or your account's region setting was updated. A full sign-out refreshes all tokens and re-establishes a clean session.

  1. Go to spotify.com in a browser and sign in.
  2. Go to your Account page â†’ scroll to the Sign Out Everywhere button.
  3. Click it â€” this signs you out of every device including the app on your phone.
  4. Reopen the Spotify app on your phone and sign back in.
  5. Try playing a song â€” this resolves most persistent playback failures that survived cache clearing.

Why Tracks Are Greyed Out (and Whether You Can Get Them Back)

"Greyed out" means Spotify still lists the track but won't play it. The cause depends on which type of grey applies.

Greyed out with a small "Unavailable" tag

The artist or label pulled this song from Spotify, or the licensing deal for it expired. This happens more often than people realize: thousands of tracks rotate off the catalog quietly each month. There is no fix — the song genuinely isn't on Spotify anymore. The only workaround is checking another service (Apple Music, YouTube Music, Tidal) which may still license it. The track will likely return at some point if licensing is renegotiated, but no timeline is published.

Greyed out with "Not available in your country"

The song is licensed in some regions but not yours. Travel and VPN can shift this temporarily — if you log in from a country where the track is licensed, you can play it in that session. Spotify's official policy is that you should match your account country to where you actually live, but practical enforcement is loose for short trips. Note: if your account country differs from your billing card country for too long, Spotify may force a reconciliation that removes access.

Greyed out only on Free tier

Some albums and singles are Premium-exclusive on launch (typically the first 2 weeks for major releases). Free users see them in search but can't play them. The track will become available to Free users automatically when the exclusivity window ends.

Greyed out in a downloaded playlist

This is different from above — the song is technically still on Spotify but your local download is broken. The download cache for that song got corrupted or expired. Toggle Download off and on for the playlist (Library › the playlist › Download toggle), or delete the cache (Settings › Storage › Delete cache, then re-download). Premium-only feature, obviously.

Greyed out everywhere even though it played yesterday

Sudden disappearance with no warning is almost always a licensing pull. Check the artist's profile — if the song is missing from their album entirely (not just greyed for you), the label removed it. If the song is on the album page but greyed in your library, your specific saved version is broken; remove and re-add it from the artist page.

Other Specific Spotify Playback Failures

Songs skip themselves the moment they start

The song shows in the player for a fraction of a second, then jumps to the next. This is almost always a corrupted local cache for that specific track. Settings › Storage › Delete cache. If it persists across multiple devices, the issue is server-side — remove and re-add the song to your library.

Spotify pauses itself every minute or two on Android

Aggressive battery management on Samsung One UI, Xiaomi MIUI, and OnePlus OxygenOS kills Spotify's audio session. Settings › Apps › Spotify › Battery › Unrestricted. Also disable any "Power saving" or "Adaptive battery" mode globally. The default settings on these phones are tuned for battery life over background audio reliability.

"Spotify can't play this right now"

Verbatim error that Spotify shows for several different underlying causes. Three things to try in order: log out and back in (clears a stale auth token), clear the app's cache (Settings › Storage › Delete cache), or try the same track on a different device on your account. If only one device fails, that device's local Spotify install is the problem. Reinstall.

Downloaded songs won't play offline

You marked a playlist for offline use, but tracks won't play when you turn Wi-Fi off. Spotify periodically requires you to "go online" once every 30 days to verify your Premium status — even for downloaded content. Connect to Wi-Fi or cellular, open Spotify, let it sync for 60 seconds, then go back offline. If that doesn't help, the download itself is corrupted: re-download the playlist.

No sound but the track is playing (progress bar moves)

Audio routing problem, not a Spotify problem. Check: phone volume up, not on silent, and Bluetooth is connected to the device you actually want sound from. The "playing on" indicator at the bottom of the Spotify screen tells you which device is currently selected — it's often a forgotten Bluetooth speaker in another room.

What NOT to Do

Common mistakes that make this worse
  • Don't log out and back in as a first step. Logging out of Spotify removes all downloaded songs (Offline mode) and resets your listening session. Clear the cache first â€” it resolves most playback failures without losing your downloads.
  • Don't download songs over mobile data if you're near your data cap. Spotify downloads are 30-300 MB per album depending on quality. Downloading over a throttled or near-cap connection can appear to succeed but produces corrupt files that won't play offline.
  • Don't assume a grey song title means the song is unavailable in your country. Grey tracks in Spotify can mean the song is unavailable in your region, OR that your local cache is corrupted. Try toggling Offline Mode off and on â€” if the track becomes playable, it was a cache issue, not a regional restriction.
  • Don't run Spotify and a VPN simultaneously without configuring them correctly. Spotify checks your region against your account country. If your VPN routes traffic through a country where a song is restricted, Spotify greyes it out even if it was available before. Disable the VPN to test playback.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why are songs greyed out on Spotify and can't be played?

A: Greyed-out songs are unavailable in your region due to music licensing restrictions â€” this isn't a bug or a fixable error. The artist's label hasn't licensed the track for streaming in your country. Look for alternate versions of the song, or import the MP3 locally through Spotify's local files feature.

Q: Why does Spotify keep pausing by itself?

A: The most common cause is another device actively using your Spotify account at the same time. Go to spotify.com, sign into your account, and click "Sign Out Everywhere" to kick all other sessions. Android battery optimization can also kill Spotify in the background â€” exclude it from battery optimization in Settings > Battery > Battery Optimization.

Q: Why does Spotify skip to the next song immediately without playing?

A: Immediate skipping is almost always a corrupted download or an expired session token. First, go to Settings > Storage > Delete Cache. If that doesn't help, log out completely (Settings > Log Out) and log back in. For a downloaded song that skips, remove the download and re-download it.

Q: Will clearing Spotify's cache delete my downloaded songs?

A: No â€” the "Delete Cache" option in Spotify's Settings > Storage only removes temporary files. Your offline downloads are stored separately and won't be affected. Reinstalling Spotify, however, will remove all downloads and require you to re-download them.

Q: Why does Spotify say "Can't play this right now"?

A: This error means the track can't be streamed at that moment. Common causes: the song isn't licensed in your region (it'll be greyed out), your Premium subscription has expired, or there's a temporary server issue. Check your subscription status at spotify.com/account and try logging out and back in to refresh your session.

Free users can submit a support ticket â€” include your device model, OS version, and Spotify version number (found in Settings > About).
